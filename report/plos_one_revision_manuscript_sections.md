## Revised abstract

Computational misinformation research has predominantly treated veracity classification as its central predictive task. Yet the practical impact of harmful information also depends on amplification and diffusion, whose measurement is not captured by a binary veracity label. We revisit this contrast using 92,969 EVONS news items and 578 processed FakeNewsNet–PolitiFact news items (434 with at least eleven recovered tweets for matched early-window analyses). We compare fixed RoBERTa and Mistral text embeddings and lightweight classifiers across two outcomes and a common grid of engagement quantiles (50th, 75th, 90th, and 95th percentiles). Evaluation uses ten repeated stratified holdout partitions with disjoint 60% fitting, 20% threshold-calibration, and 20% testing portions; we report average precision, ROC-AUC, majority-class baselines, and F1 both at the conventional and calibration-selected thresholds. At the 95th percentile on EVONS, a Mistral-based gated model achieves mean calibrated F1 = 0.378, compared with F1 = 0.262 at threshold 0.5 and average precision = 0.345 at a positive-class prevalence of approximately 0.05. FakeNewsNet experiments using the first 1, 3, 5, and 10 recovered tweets demonstrate substantial sensitivity to observation windows, but use a retrospective fixed-length cohort and retain total engagement as the trained target. Separately, a prospective-label audit excludes engagement in the observed prefix from the outcome. We conclude that diffusion-oriented evaluation depends on the engagement proxy, threshold, sample selection, and the time at which signals become observable. Importantly, the EVONS veracity label reflects source-level annotation and should not be interpreted as independent fact checking of each article.

## Materials and methods — data, tasks, and label provenance

EVONS comprises **92,969** news items in the analyzed file, including **42,776** assigned the fake-source label and **50,193** assigned the real-source label. The binary `is_fake` field records whether the originating *media source* belongs to the dataset's fake- or real-news source category. Although assigned to each record, this is not an item-by-item verification result. Consequently, we distinguish *source-associated veracity classification* in EVONS from fact-checking an article's individual claims. This distinction is particularly important when interpreting near-ceiling scores and potential source-confounding effects. The `fb_engagements` variable represents the dataset's aggregate Facebook engagement proxy. All 92,969 records in the analyzed export had a nonmissing engagement count.

For FakeNewsNet, we analyze the **PolitiFact** subset of recovered propagation sequences, rather than the complete multi-source FakeNewsNet collection. Its preprocessed JSONL files contain **578** stories (174 fake, 404 real) and **401,689** recovered tweets. The median observed propagation length is **72** tweets (range 1–22,750). A separate matched cohort retains **434** stories (143 fake, 291 real) with at least eleven recovered tweets. This restriction ensures observable data after the tenth tweet and keeps sample membership constant across the 1-, 3-, 5-, 10-tweet and full-propagation text representations. It also conditions inclusion on a later-observed property (recovered cascade length), so these results are *not* a deployment study of all stories at the time of their first tweet. These counts are sample sizes after processing; they do not estimate what fraction of all original PolitiFact stories was recovered through the platform API.

In EVONS, text models encode article titles and descriptions as separate fixed representations, compared across RoBERTa-base (`FacebookAI/roberta-base`) and the Mistral embedding API (`mistral-embed`). For FakeNewsNet, the input is the mean of the first `k` **recovered tweet-text embeddings** in chronological order, with `k = 1, 3, 5, 10`. A retrospective full-sequence baseline averages all recovered tweet embeddings. This is **not** a first-`k` article representation, nor is it an embedding of independently retrieved news-article text. Input features to these classifiers do not include tweet `favorite_count`, although this quantity supplies the virality label. The construction of cached embeddings and matching order requires independent confirmation by the data owners; numerical shape checks do not establish that provenance.

For virality classification let `E_i` denote article-level Facebook engagements (EVONS) or the total sum of observed tweet `favorite_count` (FakeNewsNet). At empirical quantile `q`, the outcome is **`y_i(q) = 1[E_i > Q_q(E)]`**, using a **strict greater-than** rule (not `>=`). Quantiles `q = .50, .75, .90, .95` allow analyses across different prevalences, but are not identical substantively across platforms. On the full EVONS cohort, cutoffs are **230**, **2,041**, **8,344.4**, and **18,886.2** Facebook engagements, yielding 46,483, 23,242, 9,297, and 4,649 positives. On all 578 processed FakeNewsNet stories, the corresponding pooled `favorite_count` thresholds are 29.5, 683.75, 9,012.5, and 34,474.0. In the **434-story matched text-model cohort**, the different thresholds are **140**, **1,942.25**, **14,313.9**, and **58,985.65**, yielding **217**, **109**, **44**, and **22** positives. The two FakeNewsNet denominators must not be confused.

## Materials and methods — evaluation and statistical analysis

The four model suites compare multilayer perceptrons, logistic regression and random forests; optional XGBoost was **not installed in the new reported training run** and therefore does not contribute scores to the revised comparisons. EVONS virality additionally uses an MLP with source-category embeddings, an MLP with train-derived source-mean engagement, and two gated text/source-engagement architectures. The source-engagement feature for an EVONS fitting item is computed leave-one-out, excluding that item's engagement; calibration and test items use source statistics derived solely from the fitting portion. Unseen sources receive the fitting-set fallback mean. This fixes direct self-contamination but does not establish cross-source generalizability.

We run **ten repeated stratified holdouts**, not conventional mutually exclusive ten-fold cross-validation. Each outer random split assigns 80% to a development portion and 20% to the test set. The development portion is itself split into fitting and calibration sets, resulting in approximately **60% fitting, 20% calibration, and 20% test** per repetition. We train each model using only the fitting set, select the decision threshold maximizing calibration F1, and compute scores on the untouched corresponding test set. We report ROC-AUC, **average precision** (the non-interpolated area summary of the precision–recall curve), positive prevalence as a random-ranking AP baseline, majority-class accuracy and F1, F1 at threshold 0.5, and calibrated F1. These values are means over repetitions, not confidence intervals and not scores on ten disjoint datasets. Our cutoff-tuning procedure optimizes F1, not probabilistic calibration in the statistical reliability-diagram sense.

For FakeNewsNet text-prefix experiments, exact split indices are reused across windows, model families and encoder variants, and the script exports cohort and partition fingerprints. Inference on repeated, overlapping splits uses paired fold differences with a Nadeau–Bengio-style variance inflation `1/n + n_test/n_fit` (approximately `0.4333` for ten 60/20/20 repetitions), two-sided Student-t reference and Holm multiplicity correction. Comparisons are grouped by dataset/task/quantile/window for model-pair tests; matched `k` versus full contrasts are controlled jointly across all four prefix lengths and model families within each FakeNewsNet task/quantile. This is **approximate**: the extra calibration stage is not fully captured by the corrected-resampled test derivation, models share much of their training data, and model rankings chosen on these same folds have selection uncertainty. We interpret these comparisons as sensitivity evidence, not definitive proof of superiority. Historical 80/20 p-values are not applicable to the revised scores.

For the separate engagement-prefix audit, the observed scalar predictor is total `favorite_count` of tweets `1, ..., k`, not a trained text classifier. Its *future-only* target is the sum of `favorite_count` of tweets with index greater than `k`; the old total-engagement target is also evaluated on the **same selected story cohort**. The principal fixed cohort includes only cascades with at least 11 recovered tweets; a varying eligible-cohort analysis is reported as sensitivity. Because the available `favorite_count` values are reconstructed snapshots (rather than verified as they stood immediately after tweet `k`), these results are retrospective evidence about prefix-derived rank association, **not a validated real-time forecast**.

## Results — sample sizes and target construction

The analyses cover 92,969 EVONS articles and 578 processed FakeNewsNet–PolitiFact stories, with 434 FakeNewsNet stories in the matched text-prefix cohort. The different engagement definitions and source/label generation procedures rule out a straightforward comparison of absolute cutoffs. Matching the quantile makes the positive class similarly rare, not the social or causal mechanism of spread. Within the matched FakeNewsNet cohort, upper-tail outcomes involve just 44 (q90) or 22 (q95) positive stories, with typically 9 or 4–5 positives in each held-out test partition; performance at these cutoffs is correspondingly unstable.

### Revised model performance

For EVONS *source-associated veracity* classification, the best recorded MLP with Mistral embeddings has mean **calibrated F1 = 0.988**, **ROC-AUC = 0.999**, and **average precision = 0.999**, on a dataset with approximately 46.0% positive labels. These high values cannot be interpreted as the independent verification of individual article claims. A source-grouped validation is needed to assess performance on previously unseen publishers.

In EVONS virality, the model with the highest **mean calibrated F1** at the median cutoff is the source-embedding MLP (F1 = **0.859**, AP = **0.924**, ROC-AUC = **0.931**). At q75, q90 and q95, the best model is the Mistral-based gated classifier: respectively **calibrated F1 = 0.666 / 0.495 / 0.378**, **AP = 0.726 / 0.497 / 0.345**, and **ROC-AUC = 0.889 / 0.880 / 0.876**. At q95, the same gated model scores **F1 = 0.262** at threshold 0.5, versus **0.378** with an independently selected threshold; its precision and recall at the selected threshold average **0.334** and **0.437**. Its AP exceeds the approximate 0.05 positive-prevalence baseline, but the thresholded task remains difficult.

FakeNewsNet veracity prediction on the fixed 434-story cohort is robust across several text-input windows, although it is not an independent article fact-checking demonstration. The best mean calibrated F1 recorded by window is **0.885** for k=1 (Mistral logistic regression), **0.903** for k=3 (Mistral logistic regression), **0.897** for k=5 (RoBERTa logistic regression), **0.910** for k=10 (RoBERTa logistic regression), and **0.899** for full propagation (RoBERTa MLP). This range supports a modest description of useful textual cues even from short recovered windows, conditioned on retrospective cohort inclusion; near-ceiling scores may reflect source, collection or claim-repetition effects.

In FakeNewsNet virality, the trained target is still **total** observed engagements of each of the 434 selected stories, and so contrasts between early-text models and full-propagation baselines do not isolate a purely future-outcome forecast. At the median cutoff, the best full-sequence calibrated F1 is **0.735** (Mistral logistic regression); the best respective k=1, 3, 5, 10 values are **0.674, 0.682, 0.678, 0.676** (possibly from different model families). At q75, the best full-sequence result reaches **0.764** (Mistral random forest), whereas the best k=1 and k=10 results reach **0.557** and **0.599**. At q90, full-sequence F1 peaks at **0.615** and at q95 at **0.574**; however, only 44 and 22 total positives, respectively, enter these analyses. Cross-quantile changes partly reflect redefinition of the target and its base rate.

### Matched future-only engagement audit (revised Fig. 2)

The distinct scalar-prefix audit answers whether the observed engagements of the first k recovered tweets rank **engagement of the later tweets**, which is excluded from the target's observed prefix. The primary fixed cohort consists of **291 real-labelled** and **143 fake-labelled** stories (all with at least eleven tweets); thresholds in this part of the analysis are computed *separately in each veracity subset*, unlike the pooled classifier target.

At q50, ROC-AUC for future engagement in the real-labelled subset increases from **0.544** at k=1 to **0.670** at k=10; the corresponding fake-labelled subset goes from **0.538** to **0.559**. At q95, real-labelled values increase from **0.674** to **0.933**, while fake-labelled values decrease from **0.622** to **0.512**. But the q95 estimates rely on just **15** future-engagement positives among 291 real stories and **8** among 143 fake stories, so trends at this threshold require strong qualification. Total- and future-outcome AUCs were computed on matching cohorts, so differences cannot be attributed to a changing set of stories; the outcome labels can nonetheless differ.

Do not describe the q95 AUC = 0.933 as proof of high-accuracy, live early misinformation triage. In addition to small numerator counts, the audit uses retrospectively recovered sequences and final-snapshot counts, while the text models use a different predictor altogether.

## Discussion and limitations — replacement wording

Three distinctions matter. First, a binary veracity proxy is not automatically equivalent to article-level truth assessment: EVONS annotations inherit the source category. Second, raw engagement, tweet favorites and cascade transmission represent different aspects of diffusion; thresholding these variables changes the predictive task, even when the same quantile is applied. Third, the temporal availability of inputs and the definition of the future outcome are essential. Prefix text can be contrasted with full-propagation text on a fixed, retrospectively selected cohort, but the target in those trained models is total engagement; the separate prefix-likes audit partially addresses outcome overlap yet remains retrospective rather than deployment-ready.

The q95 EVONS classifier retains discriminative ranking ability (ROC-AUC around .876) but its calibrated F1 (.378) indicates that identifying rare highly engaged articles remains challenging. FakeNewsNet full-propagation models can reach stronger F1 at some quantiles, but they use later information unavailable at the first tweet. When restricting features to text from the first recovered tweets, differences depend on quantile and model; some are too small to distinguish reliably. Models evaluated on q95 with only 22 pooled target positives, or 8 positives in the fake-only future-outcome audit, need substantial uncertainty caveats.

Both datasets are historically curated and subject to sampling biases. The FakeNewsNet analyzed file includes only successfully processed and recovered propagations; the analyzed 434-story prefix cohort further excludes shorter sequences based on final recovered length. The original EVONS source-proxy labels permit publisher-level confounding, so high veracity performance is **not** general evidence of verifying unseen publishers' reporting or truth of individual claims. Collected `favorite_count` snapshots cannot establish engagement available in real time after each tweet. Moreover, the new random-forest and logistic-regression baseline calculations used the optional GPU cuML backend, which is not numerically identical to the sklearn CPU implementations; the repository documents a single-split backend comparison. These limitations constrain any claim of out-of-sample causal prediction, current-platform generalization or policy readiness.
