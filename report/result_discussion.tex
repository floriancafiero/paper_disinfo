\paragraph{Discussion.}
Across 470 pairwise model comparisons spanning two datasets, two tasks, and five metrics, only 29 comparisons reach statistical significance after Holm--Bonferroni correction. All 29 originate from a single condition: EVONS virality prediction. On fake-news detection (both datasets) and on FakeNewsNet virality prediction, no model consistently outperforms any other. Differences in 10-fold cross-validation scores are too small to be reliably distinguished from sampling variance. By contrast, EVONS virality prediction reveals a clear hierarchy. The \textsc{gating\_mistral} model achieves an AUC of 0.881 and an F1 of 0.312, significantly outperforming its closest competitors on F1, recall, and AUC. Crucially, models that record high accuracy and precision (\textsc{mlp\_avg\_eng} and \textsc{mlp\_source}) reach accuracy $\approx 0.951$ and precision up to 0.67, at the cost of near-zero recall ($\approx 0.05$), effectively predicting the majority class almost exclusively due to the severe class imbalance. \textsc{gating\_bert}, despite sharing the same gated-fusion architecture as \textsc{gating\_mistral}, collapses to an F1 near zero, demonstrating that the architecture alone is insufficient: the quality and dimensionality of the underlying text representation are equally decisive. This interaction between architecture and embedding space is a recurring pattern across conditions. Certain model families appear better suited to exploit specific embedding geometries: swapping the embedding source while keeping the architecture fixed can shift performance dramatically in either direction. Overall, the results suggest that, for tasks where signals are strong and classes are reasonably balanced (fake-news detection), standard classifiers applied to pre-trained embeddings reach a performance ceiling that leaves little room for architectural differentiation. Virality prediction, with its extreme class imbalance and weaker signal, is precisely the setting where architectural choices and embedding quality jointly determine whether a model can detect the minority class at all.
